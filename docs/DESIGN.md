# Design: Lines

Generated by office-hours on 2026-09-17
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder
Approach: B (Expo app + live backend)

## Problem Statement

A night out in NYC is often a sequence of 40–50 minute sidewalk lines that end with “this bar isn’t great.” Deals live on a sandwich board you only see after you’ve already walked there. The sidewalk turns into a mob in front of a handful of doors.

Lines is a phone app a friend group opens together: check in or join a virtual line at a bar, see wait and a short briefing (capacity, specials), get a buzz when they’re close to the door. Venues get a quieter sidewalk because the queue lives on the phone. A live city map and group re-route come later, after queues are real.

## What Makes This Cool

The sidewalk goes quiet. The line still exists — it’s just not a human wall. Phones buzz: you’re 3rd. The crew can skip a mid bar *before* they pay an hour of their night.

The 10x screenshot is a live neighborhood map: wait-for-N on each door, deals as chips, the whole group on one ticket, 2–3 tap-to-switch re-routes when the wait blows up. That map is not v1.

## Premises

1. v1 is a phone app for a friend group: virtual line + briefing + buzz — not a city-wide live map.
2. The first “whoa” is skipping a 40–50 min line that ends in a mid bar.
3. A queue is fake unless someone runs the door (even a “door” screen for 1–3 venues).
4. Group-of-N is the nightlife unit. One ticket for the crew. No stranger pickup groups in v1.
5. Live NYC heat map + re-route is 10x, not the scaffold.
6. Learning project: smallest thing the crew can open on phones, not a full operator platform.

All six agreed 2026-09-17.

## Approaches Considered

- **A — Weekend PWA:** Browser queue + door page. Fastest share-by-link. Weak iPhone “buzz.” Effort S.
- **B — Expo + live backend (chosen):** Native-feeling app, party-of-N queue, live position, push/local notification when 3rd/next, filterable deals, 1–3 venues. No map. Effort M.
- **C — City map + group re-route:** Neighborhood map, wait-for-N, tap-to-switch suggestions. XL and a pretty lie until many real queues exist.

## Recommended Approach

**B**, because the moment that matters is a buzz in a pocket. A PWA sands that off on iPhone. Do not start with C.

### v1 product shape

**Guest app (Expo, iOS + Android via Expo Go to start)**

- Open app → see 1–3 bars (seed / favorite list, not all of NYC)
- Each bar: wait for your party size, capacity-ish briefing, deals
- Filter/browse by deals
- Check in / join line as a **party of N** (create or join a group with friends)
- Live place in queue
- Notification: you’re 3rd / you’re next — walk up
- Optional later: simple “check in to favorite beforehand” (holds a party in the list without implying a reservation table)

**Door screen (same app, role toggle or `/door` venue pin)**

- See the queue as parties (name/code + size), not anonymous phone numbers only
- Tap Call next / Admit / No-show
- That’s what makes the queue real

**Out of v1**

- Live heat map of NYC
- Auto-re-route / Uber-for-bars
- Public stranger groups
- Payments, cover, ID scan, full bouncer OS
- Competing with Resy/OpenTable for restaurants

### Technical sketch (not an implementation)

- **Client:** Expo (React Native) + Expo Notifications
- **Backend:** one realtime-friendly BaaS (Supabase suggested: Postgres + Auth + Realtime). Alternative: Firebase. Pick one; don’t invent a custom queue service in v1.
- **Core tables (conceptual):** `venues`, `deals`, `parties` (size, member ids), `queue_entries` (venue, party, position/status), `door_events`
- **Wait for N:** naive at first (count parties ahead, maybe + size). Honest “unknown” beats a fake 12-minute ETA.
- **Seed:** 1–3 fictional or friendly venues so the crew can demo a night without signing every bar in Manhattan

## Open Questions

- Are the first venues fictional (“Bar Atlas”) or one real place a friend can stand at?
- Group join mechanic: invite code vs same-phone “add me”?
- Check-in vs join-line: is check-in just a favorite pin, or a timed hold that expires?

## What I noticed

You didn’t pitch “an AI tool for nightlife.” You pitched your friends burning 40–50 minutes to find out a bar is mid, and a sidewalk that shouldn’t be a mob. You separated Resy/OpenTable (restaurants, tables) from bars (no host, sandwich-board deals). In the 10x riff you wanted a live NYC map *and* group re-route — then agreed the map is a lie until queues are real, and that the unit is a party of N, not a blob of strangers. That’s taste: the screenshot is the map; the product is the quiet sidewalk and the buzz.
